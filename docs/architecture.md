# Archet — architecture

Status: **draft**, decisions of 2026-11-25. Items marked *(to be validated)* are still open.

## Goal

Programming, debug and **production-test** probe, fully open source (hardware, gateware, firmware, host software):

- high-performance SWD and JTAG, with **reliable simultaneous SWO**;
- ARM, RISC-V and ESP32 (Xtensa) targets;
- USB HS (USB-C, PD) and 100 Mbit/s Ethernet with PoE+;
- **HIL platform**: the probe carries everything generic (communication, real time, safety, synchronisation); each project only adds a **simple, project-specific shield**, or Bornier modules.

Intended use: debugging, and running the production tests of a board (IO, analog levels, current draw…). It is **not** an oscilloscope nor a logic analyser: no massive fast capture, no high-speed link to a daughter board.

Manufacturing constraint: JLCPCB, **no BGA**.

Out of scope: parallel ETM/TPIU trace.

### HIL requirements

The probe implements the platform part of the Archet HIL harness specification:

- driven by the Rust/Python library and the MCP server over the Maestro protocol;
- rules executed on the board ("on trigger, after Δt, action"): ≤ 1 µs, jitter ≤ 50 ns on raw IOs;
- edge timestamping on raw IOs (10 to 20 ns range), counters, frequency and pulse width, 64 k capture events (buffer in MCU RAM);
- safe state (high impedance) at power-up, on reset and when the host goes silent; hard limits; emergency stop button;
- several boards linked by the network only, clocks synchronised over Ethernet (IEEE 1588), discovery by the host.

### SWD/JTAG engine (FPGA)

- Transaction queue executed autonomously: retry on WAIT, parity, auto-incremented block reads/writes.
- Adjustable SWDIO sampling delay, to compensate for the cable at high frequency. Realistic max frequency on the iCE40UP5K to be measured (logic fmax around 50 to 70 MHz).
- SWDIO buffer direction driven by the FPGA (SN74LVC1T45, no auto-direction parts). A 1k pull-down holds it in input while the iCE40 is unconfigured (its IOs then have weak pull-ups). nRST and IO0 are open drain through 2N7002s, as on the ESP-Prog.

### SWO (FPGA)

- UART and Manchester, baud rate detection, timestamping on the common clock.
- FIFO in SPRAM, then QSPI link and DMA into an MCU RAM buffer, then a **dedicated** USB endpoint or TCP socket.

### MCU ↔ FPGA link (QSPI)

STM32 OCTOSPI2 (port P2: PF0–PF4, PG12) in quad mode; the iCE40 is the slave. Estimated peak throughput:

| Stream | Throughput |
|---|---|
| SWD at 50 MHz (~46 cycles per 32-bit word) | ~4 MB/s |
| JTAG at 50 MHz | ~6 MB/s |
| SWO (up to ~50 Mbit/s) + timestamps | ~8 MB/s |
| Raw IO events (bursts absorbed by SPRAM) | a few MB/s |
| **Realistic peak total** | **~15–20 MB/s** |

Capacity: **quad DTR (DDR) from the start**. The STM32 OCTOSPI supports it (with its DLYB sampling delay block), and the iCE40 SB_IO cells have input and output DDR registers. 50 MB/s raw at 50 MHz, ~40 MB/s useful: that is the USB HS ceiling, beyond which the bottleneck moves to the host. Routing accordingly (matched lengths, clean clock, no stub). SDR remains a bring-up mode. Octal rejected: 4 more IOs.

Constraints from erratum ES0491 on the OCTOSPI:

- **no DQS in quad mode**: with the DQS input enabled, quad reads are corrupted, with no workaround (§2.8.8). Read sampling therefore relies on DLYB alone; there is no DQS pin to wire;
- **at least two cycles before the data phase** without DQS (§2.8.5). In quad DTR, that means two instruction bytes or one SDR address byte, which our protocol has anyway;
- **clock mode 0**: mode 3 in SDR loses one byte when a transfer is split (§2.8.7);
- no multiplexing of the two OctoSPIs through the I/O manager, which avoids the lockup of §2.8.11.

The hardware is the same for both access modes below (CLK, NCS, IO0–3, IRQ): the choice is made in firmware and gateware, **to be settled by measurement on the board**. Starting mode:

**Streaming in indirect mode + DMA**. One command per block, then the exact length streamed; the MDMA drains the OCTOSPI FIFO into SRAM. An MDMA linked list programs the OCTOSPI registers itself (command, length): no CPU in the loop *(to be confirmed on the H723)*.

- **512-byte blocks** (one USB HS packet), each with a header: stream (SWD, SWO, events), payload length, fill levels of all streams. The FPGA raises IRQ as soon as a block is full, or on timeout with a partial block (bounded latency for a quiet SWO).
- Efficiency: ~12 command, address and dummy cycles for 512 data cycles in DTR, i.e. ~98 %.
- Length known in advance, hence **destructive-read FIFOs** are allowed: no speculative or prefetch reads, unlike memory-mapped mode (which would require non-destructive windows, an MPU *Device* region and an explicit read pointer).
- Commands and registers use short indirect transactions; SWD queues go down in blocks by DMA.
- A single transfer queue on the OCTOSPI: SWD and SWO alternate by blocks, independence comes from separate FIFOs in the FPGA.
- Per-transaction latency (~1 µs) is negligible against the USB micro-frame (125 µs).

Conditions:

- **batched transactions**: the FPGA executes whole SWD queues and returns results in blocks, never one word per QSPI transaction;
- **one FIFO per stream** in the FPGA (SWD, SWO, events) and a fill-level IRQ line: the MCU drains by DMA, SWO never blocks SWD;
- iCE40UP5K fmax at 50 MHz to be checked on this block; fallback to 33 MHz (~13 MB/s) acceptable.

**Buffers: no external RAM.** The iCE40UP5K has 128 KB of SPRAM (4 independent 32 KB blocks, 16-bit) and 15 KB of EBR. Indicative split: SWO 64 KB (~8 ms at 8 MB/s), events 32 KB, SWD 32 KB; small clock-domain FIFOs in EBR. These buffers cover the MCU service latency (a few tens of µs), not storage: the 64 k events live in STM32 RAM. Sustained throughput is capped by the QSPI, not by memory; external RAM would also cost IOs the iCE40 does not have. Delta-encoded events (≈ 4 bytes) double the depth.

## Target connectors

Two connectors, both industry standards, no in-house adapter. Pinouts in [`hardware/connectors.csv`](../hardware/connectors.csv).

| Probe | Target connector | UART | Target supply |
|---|---|---|---|
| J-Link | 20-pin 2.54 mm (adapters to 10-pin 1.27 mm) | VCOM on 2 pins of the 20 | 5 V on pin 19 |
| STLINK-V3 | STDC14 (Cortex 10-pin + UART on 13–14) | yes | no, T_VCC is a sense input |
| ARM 20-pin (J-Link PRO) | 2.54 mm, nTRST, 9 grounds | — | 5 V on pin 19 (J-Link) |
| Black Magic Probe | Cortex 10-pin | separate | 3.3 V on VTref, on command |
| ESP-Prog | JTAG 2×5 + PROG 2×3 (EN, IO0, UART, VDD) | on PROG | VDD |

- **J_TARGET: STDC14** (2 × 7, 1.27 mm). SWD/JTAG, SWO, nRST, T_VCC and the UART (T_VCP_RX on pin 13 is a probe output, to the target RX). A 10-pin Cortex target plugs on the centre pins 3–12. Pin 9 (RTCK) is left unconnected: no IO left on the iCE40.
- **Pins 1–2, reserved by ST, used as on the STLINK-V3SET**: there, pin 1 carries USB 5 V when jumper JP2 is fitted (default; UM2448: "provides 5 V power on connectors, required for SWIM usage, B-STLINK-VOLT and B-STLINK-ISOL boards": ST's own add-on boards are powered from it), and pin 2 the SWDIO direction (3.3 V, high when the probe drives; community measurement, not in ST's manual). Pin 1 checked on a STLINK-V3SET (2026-11-27); pin 2 is Archet's own definition, whatever the V3SET does. ST still asks targets to leave them unconnected. Archet: **pin 1 = TGT_VSUP**, **pin 2 = SWDIO_DIR**, the iCE40 signal that already drives our SWDIO translator, copied through a 74LVC1G125 buffer and a series resistor. A board made for a STLINK-V3SET (5 V on pin 1, translator on pin 2) works on Archet. nTRST is not provided (old ARM7/9 only).
- **J_PROG: ESP-Prog PROG pinout** (2 × 3, 2.54 mm): EN, VDD, TXD, GND, RXD, IO0. An ESP32 board with this header plugs in directly; also takes Dupont wires for any board. EN is the same line as nRST, TXD/RXD the same UART as STDC14: one connector in use at a time. Checked against the ESP-Prog user guide (Program Interface table) and schematic SCH_ESP32-PROG_V2.1: ESP_TXD (pin 3) is the target TX, read by the FT2232 (FT_RXD), so it lands on TGT_UART_RX; ESP_RXD (pin 5) is driven by FT_TXD, so TGT_UART_TX. Unlike the ESP-Prog, which supplies 3.3 or 5 V on VDD, Archet only senses it: a board powered by the ESP-Prog alone needs its own supply here.
- **Target supply: adjustable 1.65–5.04 V out (translator floor, DAC buffer range), the target logic voltage comes back**, as with the J-Link pin 19 (fixed 5 V there): the probe sends **TGT_VSUP** (STDC14 pin 1), set by the firmware to what the target expects (1.8, 3.3 or 5 V, 5 V for a STLINK-V3SET board, or its regulator input), and the target returns its logic rail on T_VCC. **TGT_VCC** (T_VCC and PROG VDD) is a sense input only (ADC PF11), the reference of the translators.
  - **TPS63020 buck-boost** fed from +5V: 1.2–5.5 V out, 3 A switch, true 5 V even when +5V is in dropout on a 5 V USB port. Output disconnected in shutdown: no back-feed from a target that has its own supply. **PS/SYNC low** (power save): the converter never sinks current from a target that holds the rail higher. 1.5 µH inductor (FXL0420, Isat 6 A; 1.9 A peak at 1 A out). PG unused (no MCU pin left): the INA226 reads the output.
  - **Set** by DAC1_OUT1 (PA4) injecting into the FB divider (150 kΩ / 18 kΩ, 120 kΩ from the DAC, all preferred 0402): Vout = 0.5 + 150 kΩ × (0.5 / 18 kΩ + (0.5 − VSET) / 120 kΩ). DAC at 0 V gives 5.29 V (5.44 V worst case, under the 5.5 V rating), 3.3 V gives 1.17 V; ~1 mV per 12-bit step. The 120 kΩ loads the DAC (~15 kΩ with its buffer off), so the firmware runs it buffered: 0.2–3.1 V gives 1.42–5.04 V, and **5.04 V is the ceiling** (no unbuffered 0 V setting); 4.90–5.18 V at the 1 % and VFB corners, within 5 % of a 5 V target. R178 1 kΩ isolates the buffer from the 100 nF node (50 pF maximum load), 0.8 % gain error trimmed through the INA226. The INA226 bus voltage trims the setpoint. DAC pin in high-Z (reset, bootloader): 4.67 V, but EN on PC8 is pulled low (100 kΩ), so the converter stays off until the firmware has set the DAC. 100 nF on the DAC node: the output ramps in ~3 ms with the DAC buffer off. Simulated in [`hardware/sim/tgt_vsup.py`](../hardware/sim/tgt_vsup.py) at the tolerance corners (1 % resistors, VFB 0.495–0.505 V).
  - **Monitored** by a fourth INA226 (I2C5, address **0x45**, A1 = A0 = VS) on a 50 mΩ shunt after the output capacitors (81.9 mV full scale = 1.64 A): voltage and current, alert on overcurrent or undervoltage, limit enforced by firmware: **1 A max** (one supply pin on STDC14, 1.27 mm ribbon ~1 A per conductor), set lower per target; the TPS63020 can deliver more, the limit is software. The firmware enables TGT_VSUP only when T_VCC reads zero, and cuts it if T_VCC exceeds the setpoint (target powered from elsewhere).
- **nRST readback** (PB4, through 10 kΩ): the firmware sees a target that resets itself (watchdog, brown-out) or a reset button, and checks that its own nRST pulse reached the connector.

## Standalone programmer

Archet also works without a host, as a production flasher: firmware images and their programming recipe (target, algorithm, option bytes, verification) are loaded once over USB or Ethernet, then each press on GO programs the board on the target connector.

- **Storage: W25N02KV, 256 MB QSPI NAND on OCTOSPI1** (PF10 CLK, PF8/PF9/PE2/PF6 IO0–3, PE11 NCS with pull-up). It holds the standalone images and records SWD / SWO traces (Kymo). No SD card: no socket, no file system, no card to lose.
  - Why NAND rather than NOR: a trace at 1–2 MB/s fills 16 MB in seconds; 256 MB holds about two minutes at 2 MB/s. A single chip serves both uses, so no NOR next to it.
  - Reads go through the 2 KB page buffer (25 µs page load, 60 µs with ECC; quad reads at 104 MHz), so no memory mapping: the firmware copies page by page into RAM. That is far faster than programming a target.
  - Writes (datasheet rev. G): page program 250 µs typ / 700 µs max, block erase (128 KB) 2 / 10 ms. Recording into pre-erased blocks, a 2 KB page costs at worst 700 µs + ~80 µs of quad load, ≈ 2.6 MB/s; typically ≈ 6 MB/s.
  - Firmware duties: internal 8-bit ECC on by default; skip the factory-marked bad blocks (at least 2,008 of 2,048 valid) and retire the ones that fail later (P-FAIL / E-FAIL); program the pages of a block in order, at most 4 partial programs per page; one block per recipe, since erase is per 128 KB.
- **GO button** (PF15, EXTI), next to the existing stop button (PC13).
- **PASS and FAIL LEDs** on TIM1_CH4 (PE14) and TIM1_CH3 (PA10): one timer, brightness and blinking in PWM. Active low (cathode on the pin, anode to +3V3 through the resistor): the ROM bootloader pull-up on PA10 (USART1 RX) keeps FAIL off and the RX line idle.
- **Buzzer: passive magnetic transducer MLT-8530** (2.7 kHz resonance) on TIM17_CH1 (PB9): the timer sets the tone (short high beep for PASS, low beeps for FAIL), the duty cycle the volume. 2N7002 low-side with a gate pull-down (PB9 is I²C1 SDA in the ROM) and a 1N5819WS flyback diode, fed from +5V through a 22 Ω 1206 series resistor (~95 mA bursts kept off the +3V3 logic rail). The coil is 16 Ω, and the datasheet's 95 mA at 5 Vo-p puts its impedance near 53 Ω at resonance; 22 Ω brings it to its 3.6 Vo-p rating. With the gate held high the resistor would take 0.34 W, above its 0.25 W rating: the firmware never leaves PB9 high. TIM17 is independent of TIM4: changing the tone leaves LED_STATUS untouched.
- **LEDs**: STATUS on the MCU (PB8, TIM4_CH3, alive from reset), PASS and FAIL on the MCU (must work with no bitstream), LINK and one spare on the iCE40 current sinks RGB1/RGB2 (40, 41), written by the MCU once the FPGA runs.

## Field bus: CAN FD or RS-485

One 3-point pluggable terminal (J_FIELD, 3.81 mm, same family as VIN_EXT): H, L, GND. CAN FD and RS-485 are both a differential pair with a 120 Ω termination, so they share the terminal, the termination and the ESD; one is active at a time.

- **Uses**: drive Archet from a host or a PLC over the field bus, sniff or send frames to other devices of a HIL bench, and reach the **STM32 ROM bootloader over CAN** (FDCAN1 on PD0/PD1).
- **Transceivers**: TCAN3413 (CAN FD, 5 Mbit/s, 3.3 V supply, ISO 11898-2: mixes with 5 V nodes; independent of +5V, which sags on a 5 V USB source) and THVD2450 (RS-485 half duplex, 3.3 V, 50 Mbit/s, ±70 V bus fault: survives 24 V wired to H or L, where a MAX3485-class part stops at ±13 V; fail-safe receiver, no bias on the probe). Both TX inputs on PD1 (FDCAN1_TX or UART4_TX); RX back to PD0 through a 74LVC1G157 mux.
- **Mode: FIELD_SEL_485 (PB2), pulled down: CAN by default**, the state the ROM bootloader and an unprogrammed board see. High: mux on the RS-485 receiver and TCAN3413 in standby (STB pin on the same net), so the CAN driver never fights the RS-485 driver.
- **RS-485 direction**: DE on PG15, pulled down (driver off); receiver always on (RE tied low). CAN listen-only (sniffing) uses the FDCAN bus monitoring mode, no extra pin.
- **Termination**: 120 Ω through a slide switch (MSK-12C02), off when Archet sits in the middle of a bus. ESD: NUP2105L (24 V working voltage, covers both CAN and RS-485 common-mode range).

## Daughter boards

Two independent interfaces:

| Interface | Probe side | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Shield** (stacked or on ribbons) | iCE40 3.3 V LVCMOS raw IOs (16) + STM32 services | Project-specific shield: transceivers, level shifters, relays, measurements |
| **Bornier local bus** | STM32 | Bornier DIN-rail extension modules: slow IOs on SPI chips |

**Throughput ceiling to the host**: about 40 MB/s over USB HS and 11 MB/s over Ethernet.

### Bornier local bus

Taken from `hardware/base/gen/bus.py` in [Bornier](https://github.com/bornier-dev/bornier):

- **IDC 2 × 17** connector, ribbon cable to DIN-rail modules;
- SPI 8 MHz, mode 0; **CS0 to CS11 shifted by each module** (barrel shift), up to 12 chained modules with no address configuration;
- I²C, IRQ, active-low OE, **LATCH** (on Bornier: EtherCAT Distributed Clocks SYNC0);
- +3V3_BUS limited to ~0.5 A; +VIN_BUS1 and +VIN_BUS2, supply voltage (12 or 24 V), 480 mA each, current-limited switch per pin;
- 49.9 Ω source series resistors, pulls holding CS, LATCH and OE inactive, ESD at the connector.

Adaptation to the probe:

- **driven by the STM32**, as on Bornier; **LATCH** on an STM32 timer slaved to the synchronised clock (IEEE 1588);
- **VIN_BUS**: with a 24 V input (VIN_EXT, 10.8–24 V), straight through the limited switches. On USB or PoE, a **24 V boost converter** takes over, current-limited to the remaining budget. The boost is turned off as soon as a real 24 V input is present;
- **+VIN_BUS1, +VIN_BUS2**: one **TPS27S100B** per ribbon pin (40 V, 80 mΩ, enable from the MCU, auto-retry). Limit **525 mA** (R_ILIM 4.7 kΩ, ±28 %) rather than Bornier's 480 mA: TI recommends a limit of 0.5 A or more. DIAG_EN tied high: protections and IMON always on. The two IMON current outputs (I/500) join on one 2 kΩ load, 3.84 V at 0.96 A, under the 4 V linear limit; half of it goes to PF7 (1.92 V full load). A fault pulls IMON to 4.3–4.9 V, read 2.15–2.45 V: total current and fault on one ADC input;
- **+3V3_BUS**: TPS2553 from +3V3, 505–616 mA limit (R_ILIM 47 kΩ), enable from the MCU (PD4, pulled low), open-drain FAULT on PD7 with a red LED;
- **module identification**: one I²C EEPROM per module, with one address pin (A0) tied to the module CS (its local CS0, after shifting). Only the selected module answers the read address. The probe scans CS0 to CS11 and reads each EEPROM: it gets the number of modules, their position and their descriptor. Constraints:
  - addresses 0x50 and 0x51 reserved on the whole bus (all unselected modules answer on the other one);
  - 8-pin EEPROM whose address pins are sampled at every start condition (to be checked per part);
  - pulling a CS low without SPI clock must not trigger anything on the module (beware of ADCs starting a conversion on the CS edge);
- the Bornier schematic block (`bus.py`, `kicadgen` generator) is reused: same pinout, terminations, pulls and ESD grouping (sheet "Local bus", block of the same name in `hardware/gen/circuit.py`).

### Board

**4 layers** (JLCPCB, JLC04161H-3313 stack-up), decision of 2026-11-27: USB HS 90 Ω and Ethernet 100 Ω differential pairs, ULPI and QSPI at 50–60 MHz over a continuous ground plane, 432 parts on ~150 cm².

Net classes and custom DRC rules are generated by `hardware/gen/netclasses.py` (justifications in the script): Default 0.15/0.15 mm, power by current (1 A, 3 A), field bus and PoE primary at 0.6 mm (IPC-2221B B2, 31–100 V), impedance classes (USB 90 Ω, Ethernet 100 Ω, 50 Ω single-ended), and a 2 mm PoE isolation barrier in `archet.kicad_dru` (the magjack and the DP5700 keep their own pad geometry). Both rules are proven active by a faulty DRC. Parts whose own land pattern puts pads of different nets closer than their class (power MOSFET gate beside source or drain, QFN exposed pads) keep that geometry too, pad to pad only: rule "Tight parts, own pads", list computed by the script. Widths are for JLC04161H-3313 (0.1 mm prepreg: 50 Ω at 0.16 mm, narrower than the ULPI, QSPI and RMII pads; 7628 would need 0.35 mm) and must be confirmed with the JLCPCB impedance calculator. Power rails are tracks, not zones (decision of 2026-11-27): 0.9 mm for 3 A, necked down only to enter the 110 pads narrower than that (down to 0.24 mm, HSOP-8 and SOT-23-6), by a `track_width` rule (min 0.2 mm, opt 0.9 mm) proven active by a faulty DRC. The router's neck-down is pending.

**Outline** (decision of 2026-11-27): a rectangle with rounded corners and M3 standoffs at the corners, shared with the shield, **100 × 150 mm**: the courtyards of the 434 parts add up to 71.4 cm², a 0.48 fill. **Host side and target side on the two short edges**, so the signal runs along the board (USB and Ethernet, MCU, FPGA, target): J3 (RJ45 PoE), J1 (power), J2 and J5 (USB-C) on one; J9 (field bus), J7 (STDC14) and J8 (ESP-Prog) on the other. J12 (local bus, 51.9 mm) goes on the bottom long edge at the host end (decision of 2026-11-28): it takes a ribbon to the Bornier, like the host cables. The shield headers J10 and J11 go on both long edges at the target end (decision of 2026-11-28). J4 (TC2030) and J6 (console) stay inside.

**Board file** (`hardware/gen/board.py`, 2026-11-28): generated from a fresh netlist export, overwritten only with `--force` once placement starts. 4 layers, 1.6 mm, outline with 4 mm corner radius, four NPTH 3.2 mm standoff holes on the radius centres. ### Shield connectors

**Two IDC 2 × 17 headers, the same part as the Bornier bus** (shrouded and keyed, C20920): one part for the three connectors, a 34-way ribbon or a direct plug. A simple shield can be built on perfboard. Pinout of all three: [`hardware/connectors.csv`](../hardware/connectors.csv).

**The shield stacks on top of the probe** (decision of 2026-11-27): J_SHIELD_IO (J10) and J_SHIELD_SVC (J11) on the two long edges at the target end, so its area does not add to the probe's. **The shield covers the target end** (decision of 2026-11-28): it keeps clear of the DP5700 (13.0 mm, courtyard x 23.5–44.9, y 15.1–47.9 in the board frame of the Board section) and of J3 (13.45 mm, x up to 21.0), and of the user interface (LEDs, GO, STOP, buzzer, x 14–49): it stops beyond x ≈ 50 and uses the two target-corner standoffs. The shield plugs in with 2 × 17 female headers on its underside, through-hole, 8.5 mm body (HC-PM254-8.5H-2x17PZ, C22373922): its 5.0 × 43.6 mm body enters the 6.57 × 48.5 mm shroud of C20920, which guides it, and the pins align the boards. Board-to-board height = shroud floor height + 8.5 mm. The C20920 drawing does not dimension the floor; scaled within its 8.8 mm shroud on the end view, the floor stands 2.5 mm and the pin tips 6.3 mm above the board. **11 mm M3 standoffs** (decision of 2026-11-27): the female seats on the floor, or floats up to a few tenths above it with ~3.5 mm of pin still engaged, so a floor error of ±0.3 mm does not matter. Under the shield, parts stay below 9 mm (11 mm less 2 mm for the tails of the shield's through-hole parts). The headers stay male and shrouded, so a ribbon still serves a remote shield. Under the shield, nothing stands taller than the standoff height.

- **J_SHIELD_IO**: the 16 raw IOs (iCE40 3.3 V LVCMOS, 33 Ω series, ESD at the header), each on an even pin between two grounds, as on Bornier: conductor n = pin n, so every IO has a ground on each side in the ribbon. Pins 33–34 are grounds.
- **J_SHIELD_SVC**: services, then power.

Signals on even pins 2–22, a ground on each odd pin; rail groups separated by a column of grounds (pins 25–26, 31–32), VIN_EXT last with a ground as its only ribbon neighbour.

**Crossed ribbon** (a shield cable on the Bornier header or the reverse), all three headers being identical:

- J_SHIELD_IO pins 33–34 are grounds: they meet VIN_BUS, which the 525 mA switches limit;
- 3V3_OUT comes on when SHIELD_PRESENT_N goes low, budgeted by the firmware to a few mA until a valid descriptor is read on I2C4: it powers the ID EEPROM. 5V_OUT stays off until then. On the Bornier header, the shield I²C pins face CS lines: no descriptor, no 5V_OUT; 3V3_OUT pin 23 meets a ground there, and the eFuse trips and flags it;
- VIN_BUS and +3V3_BUS faults are limited by their switches; the other lines are 3.3 V logic on both sides, behind series resistors;
- silkscreen names each header; a second key would need a different part.

## Power

### Sources

Four sources ORed by ideal diodes into VSYS (4.5–24 V). A single wide-input buck makes +5V, from which the logic and shield rails are derived; the VIN_BUS boost is fed straight from VSYS:

```
USB-C PD VBUS (5–20 V) ── MX74700T ─┐
USB-C HS VBUS (5 V)    ── MX74700T ─┤  (after the Q11 soft start)
PoE module (12 V)      ── MX74700T ─┼── VSYS ─┬─ LMR33630 ── +5V ───┬── TLV62569 ── +3V3      (probe)
VIN_EXT (10.8–24 V)    ┬─ MX74700T ─┘ SMBJ24CA│  (36 V, 3 A)        ├── TLV62569 ── +3V3_AUX ─── eFuse ── 3V3_OUT (shield)
                       │                      │                     ├── TPS63020 ── TGT_VSUP 1.65–5.04 V (target, STDC14 pin 1)
                       │                      │                     └── eFuse ── 5V_OUT (shield, if VSYS ≥ 6 V)
                       │                      └─ LM5155 boost ── 24 V ──┐  (disabled in hardware
                       └── MX74700T ────────────────────────────────────┴── VIN_BUS switches     when VIN_EXT is present)
```

**VIN_EXT is limited to 24 V** (Archet runs on a bench, from a stable supply; 12 or 24 V nominal as on Bornier). A 5 A fuse (12H1500C) then the SMBJ24CA TVS protect it. The TVS is bidirectional: a reversed supply only meets the MX74700T (anode −90 V) instead of conducting through the TVS. It starts conducting at 26.7 V and clamps at 38.9 V: below the 40 V of the TPS27S100 and the 45 V of the LM5155.

- **Priority: highest voltage wins**, with no control logic: VIN_EXT 24 V > PD 15–20 V > PoE 12 V > USB 5 V in the usual cases. A forced priority would need back-to-back FETs per source (an ideal diode with EN low still conducts through the body diode of its FET); none of the cases below justifies it. The firmware identifies the active source from the ADC inputs (VIN_EXT, PD VBUS, PoE 12 V, HS port CC) and the VSYS INA226, and derives the budget from it.
- **One ideal diode per source**, both USB ports included: a sink P-MOS alone would back-feed the host VBUS through its body diode as soon as VSYS exceeds 5 V.
- **MX74700T** (Wuxi Maxin Micro, C22468156): pin-compatible with the TI LM74700 (same KiCad symbol and SOT-23-6), same figures (datasheet V10: V(AK REV) −17 mV max, tReverse 0.75 µs max, 2.37 A gate sink), anode ±90 V operating, cathode–anode 75 V; $0.41 against $2.61, 45,000 in stock. Simulated with the LM74700 figures (`oring.py`). VCAP 1 µF 50 V 0603 (0.1 µF minimum): ~0.5 µF left at the 12–13 V VCAP–ANODE bias. The 300 µA pump then needs ~22 ms to reach its 6.6 V UVLO at power-up, on the body diode meanwhile, as the simulations assume.
- **LMR33630 rather than TPS5430**: the TPS5430 needs 5.5 V and would not run on a 5 V USB port. 36 V, 1.5 × the 24 V maximum: the SMBJ24CA only clamps at 38.9 V at its full rated surge current (15.5 A), an EMC test case; on a bench it only conducts on hot-plug ringing, at low current, around 27–30 V. No basic or preferred buck fits (TPS54331: 28 V, too close to the TVS breakdown). Synchronous, it runs up to ~98 % duty: on 5 V USB, +5V is 4.6 V typical and 4.1 V worst case ([`hardware/sim/buck5.py`](../hardware/sim/buck5.py)), enough for the TLV62569 (2.5–5.5 V); 5V_OUT stays off.
- **+3V3_AUX separate from +3V3**: a shield or target short never pulls the probe rail down.
- **VIN_BUS**: VIN_EXT through a fourth ideal diode (U52 MX74700T + Q10, < 10 mW at 1 A; ~2 µA reverse leakage, where a Schottky would lift VIN_EXT_P from the boost output and fake a VIN_EXT), or the boost when VIN_EXT is absent. The boost enable is gated in hardware by VIN_EXT presence: with a 12 V VIN_EXT, the 24 V boost would otherwise take over.
- **Boost: LM5155 controller** (3.5–45 V input), external 60 V N-MOS, Schottky and sense resistor. Fed from VSYS, it takes the whole source budget: no detour through the +5V rail. Current sense sized for ~4 A peak: ~0.5 A at 24 V from 5 V, ~1 A (the two VIN_BUS pins) from 12 V and above. 10 µH inductor FXL1040-100-M (Isat 8.5 A), 15 mΩ sense, 24.5 V out, ~430 kHz; loop and switching simulated by `hardware/sim/boost.py`. Disabled, it passes VSYS to VIN_BUS through its inductor and diode: with a 20 V PD charger and a 12 V VIN_EXT, VIN_BUS is then fed by the charger, which the VSYS INA226 counts. The integrated MT3608B and SX1308 stop at 24 V input, with no margin; the TPS55340 has too little stock.
- **Switchover**: losing the active source falls back to the next one with no interruption, but the budget may collapse (VIN_EXT unplugged with a 10 W shield, fallback on a 500 mA USB port). The firmware watches the active source on the ADC and cuts 5V_OUT, 3V3_OUT and VIN_BUS within milliseconds; the host port's own protection covers the rest.

1. **VIN_EXT** (10.8 to 24 V), through the shield or a power terminal block: also powers the probe, including on Ethernet without PoE, and goes straight to VIN_BUS;
2. **PoE+** 802.3at class 4 (25.5 W at the PD), through an **integrated isolated SDAPO PD module**: DP5700-12V2A (24 W, documented 802.3at compliance); the DP9700 "at" is out of stock at JLCPCB (2026-11-25). To check:
   - TYP2-DET tells an at PSE (25.5 W) from an af one (13 W): a switch and 27 kΩ to VIN-, closed under a Type 2 PSE (the DP5700 copies the Silvertel Ag5700, datasheet §2.2.3, figure 4), carried to PC15 by an optocoupler;
   - input circuit as in the datasheet: MB10S bridges on both pair sets, SMAJ58A, RJ45 line-side centre taps on pins 7–10;
3. **USB-C, two ports**. A host port rarely gives more than 5 V (laptops included), so the 480 Mbit/s link and PD are split:
   - **J2, PD and FS**: STUSB4500 sink up to 20 V, D+/D− to the internal FS PHY (PA11/PA12): ROM DFU, or a 12 Mbit/s port for the firmware. The power input for a PD charger. The sink switch Q4 closes at attach on 5 V with a Miller soft start (C141 + R181, C134 1 µF): 0.27 A peak into VSYS, within the default current of a USB 2.0 port ([`hardware/sim/vbus_switch.py`](../hardware/sim/vbus_switch.py));
   - **J5, HS, 5 V only**: Rd 5.1 kΩ on both CC lines, D+/D− to the USB3320C. A self-biased P-MOS soft start (Q11, no driver) keeps the ~90 µF of VSYS under the USB 2.0 attach limit (10 µF / 50 µC): on 13 ms after attach, 88 mA peak ([`hardware/sim/usb_hs_inrush.py`](../hardware/sim/usb_hs_inrush.py)). D21 (gate to GND) re-arms it when VBUS falls: a hub cycling the port finds Q11 off again. The CC lines are averaged into PA6 (R7/R8 100 kΩ): 0.51 × vRd, < 0.34 V default USB, 0.34–0.63 V 1.5 A, above 3 A. The Rd seen by the source stays at 4.98 kΩ (4.59–5.61 kΩ allowed);
   - a PD controller on J5 too would cost ~$4 and a second I²C device for a case (a PD charger on the data port) that J2 already covers;
   - current advertised on CC without PD: 0.5 A on a USB-A port, 1.5 or 3 A on a USB-C port (up to 15 W);
   - **USB PD** (independent of the USB 2.0 data rate, goes through CC): up to 20 V on a charger, hence the "PD charger + Ethernet without PoE" case;
   - v1: standalone PD controller (STUSB4500 preferred, in stock; AP33772 low stock; CH224K out of stock), negotiating before the MCU boots; the H723 has no UCPD: the PD controller remains sole master of CC, driven over I²C;
   - on a 500 mA USB-A port, the probe runs without powering the target or the shield;
   - PDOs programmed in the STUSB4500 NVM: 5 V, 15 V, 20 V at the highest current the charger offers (up to 3 A, 60 W); the firmware reads the negotiated contract over I²C and takes it as the budget. Useful up to ~45 W: probe, 11.5 W of shield on +5V and a fully loaded VIN_BUS.

### Output to the shield

- **5V_OUT** and **3V3_OUT**, separate from VIN_EXT: no bidirectional rail.
- Two levels of limit: an eFuse per rail with a **fixed hardware ceiling** (5V_OUT 2 A, 3V3_OUT 1 A, set by resistor), and the **budget enforced by firmware** below it, from **INA226** monitors: VSYS (total drawn from the source, VIN_BUS and target included), 5V_OUT, 3V3_OUT, and TGT_VSUP for the target. On overrun the firmware disables the rail through the eFuse EN. The probe enforces the budget, it does not merely document it.
- Available budget = active source − measured VSYS power, capped by the +5V rail. It is exposed to the shield through an I2C register and to the host.
- A shield exceeding the probe budget **must** use its own supply and provide VIN_EXT. In that case, 5V_OUT is switched off.

### Safety rules for shield design

- The probe puts all shield IOs in high impedance at power-up, on reset and when the host goes silent. The shield guarantees its safe state with pull resistors. SHIELD_OE_N is pulled up to the probe +3V3 (R29), not to +3V3_OUT: a self-powered shield stays disabled while +3V3_OUT is off. Its OE_N input must accept 3.3 V while unpowered (Ioff buffer), 0.33 mA at most.
- Overcurrent shutdown is **local and in hardware** on the shield. It never depends on the link with the probe.
- The ≤ 10 µs shutdown on a real-time rule is commanded by the FPGA rule engine, through a raw IO.

### Accepted limits and firmware rules

From the schematic review of 2026-11-27:

- **+5V and 5V_OUT on a 5 V USB source** sit at 4.1–4.6 V (cable drop, buck in dropout at 98 % duty); 5V_OUT stays off there anyway.
- **TGT_VSUP** ceiling 5.04 V nominal, 4.90 V worst case (buffered DAC range), floor 1.65 V (translators).
- **TVS clamp**: the SMBJ24CA clamps at 38.9 V only at its full surge current, above the 36 V of the LMR33630; accepted for a bench supply (see Sources). On J2 it starts conducting at 26.7 V, under the 28 V absolute maximum of the STUSB4500 VBUS pin only at low current: only a faulty charger gets there.
- **Two USB ports at 5 V**: both feed VSYS through their ideal diodes and either can carry the load. The firmware budgets the smaller of the two announced currents (STUSB4500 contract on J2, CC level on PA6 for J5).
- **TGT_VSUP 1 A and STOP** are firmware limits (INA226 alert, GPIO). A hung MCU is reset by the IWDG; the PC8 pull-down then turns U25 off.
- **PoE MPS**: R14 loads the 12 V output with 12 mA (DP5700 minimum 10 mA), only ~4 mA at 48 V on the PSE side against the 10 mA 802.3 maintain power signature. To measure on the prototype with VIN_EXT winning the OR-ing: if the PSE drops power, the fallback to PoE is no longer seamless.
- **IMON** of the VIN_BUS switches saturates above ~1 A total; the fault level (2.15–2.45 V at PF7) stays distinct.
- **SWCLK, TDI and UART_TX** are always driven by their translators once TGT_VCC is present, also with the probe idle.
- **RS-485**: no fail-safe bias on the probe. The THVD2450 receiver reads an idle or open bus as high; other nodes may still need the bias of a terminated node.
- **Back-powering rule**: the firmware keeps every line to the Bornier bus low or high-Z while +3V3_BUS is off, and every shield line while +3V3_OUT is off. The pull-ups already go to those rails, not to +3V3.

## Software principle

- The probe firmware is a **generic tunnel** to the shield: register access and streams. It knows no shield in particular.
- A shield's logic lives in its optional MCU and in a host-side driver, selected by the ID EEPROM.

ST states ULPI is supported on all packages through this path (ST forum, moderator, January 2026), with a **1 mA** limit on `_C` pins; ST recommends the UFBGA144, which brings PC2/PC3 out directly (excluded: BGA). DIR and NXT are inputs on the MCU side, the limit is not reached in operation. Precautions:

- never configure PC2/PC3 as outputs;
- PHY and MCU on the same 3.3 V (no current injection if one is powered without the other);
- PHY held in reset by the MCU until the switch is closed and the pins are configured;
- IO compensation cell enabled, VERY_HIGH speed (ST recommendation);
- software workaround for the boot lockup reported on H750 (STP, DIR, NXT stuck high; released by toggling PC2SO).

Residual risk: reports of dead ULPI PHYs on H723/H750 with no established cause. To be validated on the prototype; fallback: USB FS (CMSIS-DAP compatibility) and Ethernet for performance.

Errata (read 2026-11-25): ES0491 rev 9 (H72x/73x) has no entry on `_C` pins or on ULPI; its only OTG_HS entry concerns host mode through a hub. The 1 mA limit comes from ES0445 rev 7 (H747, §2.3.10), which classifies it as a documentation erratum: "current sunk or sourced by Pxy_C pins is limited to 1 mA when the analog switch … is closed", with no workaround required. Same erratum: no Ethernet MII on these packages, RMII only (our choice).

Choices made at assignment:

- **iCE40 configured through the OCTOSPI2 pins** (single-line indirect write: CLK → SPI_SCK, NCS → SPI_SS, IO0 → SPI_SI, IO1 → SPI_SO), no separate configuration SPI. NCS and CLK switch to GPIO for the 8 clocks with SS high that precede the bitstream. The FPGA QSPI pins are therefore its configuration pins, and only CRESET_B and CDONE remain dedicated. No configuration flash. Pin numbers (SI 17, SO 14, SCK 15, SS 16) match the KiCad symbol `ICE40UP5K-SG48ITR`.
- **iCE40 supplies**: core 1.2 V from an ME6211C12 LDO on **+5V**, so VCC and VCCPLL reach 0.5 V before SPI_VCCIO1 and VPP_2V5 (UltraPlus datasheet §4.5; to confirm on the prototype); VCCPLL through 100 Ω + 10 µF + 100 nF as on the Lattice UltraPlus development platform, even with the PLL unused; VPP_2V5 on +3V3 (NVCM unused); CRESET_B pulled down, CDONE and SHIELD_OE_N (RGB0, open drain) pulled up.
- **USB3320C**: the C variant (not the USB3300) because RESETB low suspends it with every ULPI output high-Z, CLKOUT included (PA5 is SPI1_SCK of the ROM bootloader); RESETB has no internal pull, R33 holds it low. External 1.8 V from an ME6211C18 LDO on **+5V**, so VDD18 rises before +3V3 (absolute maximum: VDDIO 0.7 V while VDD18 is off). 24 MHz crystal, REFSEL[2:0] tied high; VBUS sensed through RVBUS 10 kΩ (VBUS_HS_CONN, the J5 port, 5 V; 20 V by mistake); ID tied to +3V3 (device only).
- **LAN8742A**: REF_CLK Out mode (nINTSEL low through 10 kΩ): its own 25 MHz crystal, 50 MHz REF_CLK driven to PA1, since MCO1 (PA8) is taken. nINT is then unavailable, so PG7 is free and the link state is polled over MDIO. R42 (10 kΩ) holds nRST low until PG6 drives it; the straps are latched at that edge and left to the internal pulls (MODE = 111, PHYAD = 0, REGOFF low: internal 1.2 V regulator). 49.9 Ω terminations to the centre tap, fed from +3V3 through a ferrite bead; RBIAS 12.1 kΩ 1 %; MDIO pull-up 1 kΩ. RJ45 LEDs: anodes driven by LED1/LED2 through 120 Ω (~9 mA), cathodes to GND.
- **Three I²C buses**: internal (I2C5: STUSB4500, INA226), Bornier (I2C1), shield (I2C4). An external bus fault (ribbon cable, unknown shield) never cuts the power management.
- **CS0–CS10 on port E (PE0, PE1, PE3–PE10, PE15), CS11 on PG7**: a CS is still selected with one BSRR write. PE2 and PE11 went to the QSPI flash (OCTOSPI1 IO2 and NCS have no other pin left free).
- **LATCH on TIM2_CH1 (PA15)**, 32-bit timer; its phase is reset every PTP second by ETH PPS, TIM2 internal trigger ITR4 (RM0468 table 355, slave reset mode), with no wire. The PPS (PG8) is also looped back through R175 to TIM5_CH1 (PA0, 32-bit) for an input capture of its edge: TIM2_CH1 is taken by LATCH, and ETR is no capture input.
- **FPGA reference clock on MCO2 (PC9)**, from PLL2P: frequency chosen in firmware, same crystal as the MAC.
- LSE unused: PC13–PC15 (low-speed pads, 3 mA) only carry slow inputs (stop button, shield presence, PoE at).
- JTAG disabled (SWD only): PA15 and PB4 are regular GPIOs. PB4 (NJTRST) keeps its pull-up from reset until the firmware reconfigures it: harmless on the nRST readback, which idles high; this is why the buzzer is not on PB4.

### Programming a board with no debug probe

Goal: someone who receives an Archet and owns no debug probe can still program it. A blank H723 does not start the ROM bootloader on its own (AN2606 pattern 10: BOOT0 high only, no empty-flash check), so BOOT0 must be raised in hardware.

- **GO is also BOOT0**: SW2 pulls GO_BTN to +3V3 (R68 pull-down), and R24 (1 kΩ) carries it to BOOT0 (R21 22 kΩ pull-down: 3.16 V). BOOT0 is only sampled at reset, so the button keeps its GO role once the firmware runs: GO is active high (rising edge), and GO held at power-up or reset enters the ROM bootloader. No separate BOOT0 button.
- **J2, USB-C PD/FS port, on PA11/PA12** (ROM DFU). Any USB host powers the board through it (5 V, 150–250 mA in the bootloader). GO held at plug-in, then STM32CubeProgrammer or `dfu-util`. In normal use J2 is a 12 Mbit/s port for the firmware. The FS PHY and ULPI share the one OTG_HS controller: one or the other at a time.
- **J6, 1×6 pads at 2.54 mm, FTDI cable pinout**: GND, BOOT0 (CTS position, test pad), VCC 5 V (through a Schottky to VSYS; R177 sinks its reverse leakage when the pads are open), CONSOLE_RX (PD9, cable TXD), CONSOLE_TX (PD8, cable RXD), NRST through a 1N5819WS Schottky, cathode on the cable side (cable RTS or adapter DTR): RTS low pulls NRST low, RTS high at 5 V is blocked, since NRST is not 5 V tolerant and keeps its internal pull-up. USART3 on PD8/PD9 is a ROM bootloader interface and the console in normal use. stm32flash (`-i` sequence for the reset, `-g` to start the application without dropping BOOT0) or stm32loader drive it. Needs an adapter whose VCC is 5 V **and supplies 250 mA**: the FTDI TTL-232R-5V cable is limited to 75 mA. Otherwise leave its VCC open and power the board from J2, J5 or VIN_EXT.
- **J4 (SWD)** remains the full recovery path.

**Bootloader interface selection.** The ROM polls every interface and keeps the first that shows activity (AN2606 rev 71, §62, figure 79): FDCAN frame, 0x7F on a USART, I²C address 0x5C, SPI synchronisation byte, then USB cable. AN2606 §4.4 asks for the RX lines of unused interfaces and D+/D− at a known level, no floating. The ROM also drives some pins as outputs. Survey of every ROM pin against its use on Archet:

| ROM interface | Pins (direction in the ROM) | Use on Archet | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| USART1 | PA10 RX (pull-up), PA9 TX | LED_FAIL, EFUSE_3V3_FLT | FAIL LED active low: the pull-up keeps it off and RX idle. TX high into an open-drain fault line held high: no contention while the eFuse is off |

Rules that follow, for every later sheet:

- every part that can drive an MCU pin stays in reset until the firmware releases it: **USB3320C and Ethernet PHY in reset by a pull on their reset line**, iCE40 by CRESET_B pulled low;
- every enable driven by the MCU (eFuses, target supply, boost, VIN_BUS, 3V3_BUS) has a pull to its off state: the MCU pins are high-Z in reset and in the bootloader;
- J5 (ULPI) and J2 (FS) are not used together while the ROM selects (AN2606 §4.4: no USB host on the DFU port while the other interfaces are polled; the J6 path needs J2 unplugged, or powered by a charger with no data).
